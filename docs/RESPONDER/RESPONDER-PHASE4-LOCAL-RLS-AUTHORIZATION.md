# Responder Phase 4: disposable local RLS boundary

This phase is a PostgreSQL 17.10/PostGIS 3.6.2 fixture only. `004_phase4_rls_authorization_apply.sql` is outside production migrations. It creates no production policy, Auth integration, public RPC, dashboard, publication, consumer projection, or email flow. The agency publishing gate remains false.

## Server-bound synthetic session context

The fixture connects as distinct synthetic PostgreSQL `LOGIN` roles. The trusted owner creates each role and binds its role name to a synthetic Phase 2 user UUID in the private `phase4_session_bindings` table. Authorization helpers derive the actor from PostgreSQL `session_user`; client-set custom GUCs, row columns, and supplied UUIDs cannot select a different actor. Agency and Gridly governance roles inherit separate non-login fixture groups. A normal agency role cannot switch into another actor or the governance group. Phase 2 `local_auth_identities` supplies live session-active, eligibility, and aal2 status. Each agency policy also checks the current active membership and verified/active organization, so stale role claims do not survive suspension, revocation, or org suspension.

Three scalar lookup helpers and the bounded governance organization function are `SECURITY DEFINER`, with a fixed `pg_catalog` search path, schema-qualified table references, no caller-supplied actor identity, and `PUBLIC` execution revoked. This is necessary in the local fixture to avoid recursive RLS lookups against memberships and organizations. The trusted fixture owner is a PostgreSQL superuser and can bypass RLS even with `FORCE`; ordinary synthetic login roles cannot. These helpers and the localhost trust-authenticated cluster are **not** a production Auth or RLS design. Phase 5 and any later deployment must map identity from server-verified `auth.uid()` and vetted session/AAL claims, recheck live membership and organization state in the database, avoid user-editable JWT metadata or stale role claims, review definer ownership and grants, and test real connection roles without copying this fixture unchanged.

## Coverage and read boundaries

All 13 private tables have `ENABLE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` and `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY`: `organizations`, `organization_memberships`, `organization_authorities`, `agency_updates`, `agency_update_events`, `organization_verification_events`, `organization_governance_events`, `organization_invites`, `agency_operation_receipts`, `agency_program_controls`, `local_auth_identities`, `county_geometry_catalog`, and `phase4_session_bindings`. Five agency `SELECT` policies cover organizations, memberships, current authorities, invites, and updates. There are no ordinary agency write policies or INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE/TRUNCATE grants.

Agency members at aal2 may read only their verified, active organization. VIEWER and RESPONDER see their own membership row; SUPERVISOR and AGENCY_ADMIN see the own-org roster through a limited field projection. Only AGENCY_ADMIN sees own-org invitation metadata; `token_digest` is never granted or projected, and no raw token column exists. Current approved/effective authority rows reveal FIPS, version, and dates but not geometry or provenance; revoked/history rows remain private. VIEWER sees active and terminal own-org operational updates, but not drafts or pending review. RESPONDER additionally sees their own draft/pending rows. SUPERVISOR and AGENCY_ADMIN see the own-org review queue. An active row past its expiry is labeled `expired` in the view without changing stored history. No role sees another organization's private rows.

Five read views—`responder_organization_context`, `responder_membership_roster`, `responder_current_authority`, `responder_invite_summary`, and `responder_update_queue`—use PostgreSQL `security_invoker=true`. Underlying column grants are limited to fields in those views, and the caller's RLS policies still apply to direct base-table reads. Raw update, governance, verification, and receipt events, program controls, identity rows, geometry catalog, and session bindings have no ordinary SELECT grant or policy. Bounded audit history and program-control governance interfaces remain future work. GRIDLY_ADMIN does not inherit agency access; its only Phase 4 organization read is the explicit private `phase4_governance_organization_context()` fixture function, gated by a live aal2 governance identity. It has no raw organization-table or agency-view grant.

## Write, isolation, and vector certification

The Phase 4 fixture checks all four direct SQL operations—INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE, TRUNCATE—against each of the ten original private business tables as an ordinary agency login, plus absence of the corresponding privileges. Append-only audit protections remain active for trusted setup. Four agency roles are tested against another organization's organization row, membership, invite, authority, draft, and pending update. Raw audit and receipts are inaccessible, so cross-org data in those tables cannot be read. Suspended/revoked members, a suspended organization, inactive sessions, aal1 users, and unknown identities are denied while fixture history remains stored.

`reports/responder/responder-phase4-vector-map.json` preserves nine previously complete Phase 0 vectors. N03 and N04 have RLS cross-org subconditions tested here, but their frozen actions are `create_draft` and `activate_non_closure`. Those commands, their bounded outcomes, business events, and receipt claims are not implemented until Phase 5. Accordingly, **zero new full frozen vectors** are claimed: 39 remain deferred to Phase 5, two to dashboard work, and four to consumer work. This preserves the frozen action semantics rather than treating an RLS read denial as a complete command vector.

The test harness creates a unique localhost database in a `gridly-responder-phase4-*` Temp cluster, verifies the server's actual data directory, strips inherited Supabase and PostgreSQL connection variables, uses only synthetic identities, applies Phases 1–4, reuses the certified 254-county loader, and drops the schema, database, and synthetic roles. The operator then stops the cluster and deletes only its verified Temp directory. No remote service is contacted.
